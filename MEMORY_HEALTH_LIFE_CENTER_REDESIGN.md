# Memory Health Life Center Website Redesign

## Codex Master Design and Implementation Specification

You are the lead product designer, healthcare UX strategist, nonprofit fundraising strategist, visual designer, content architect, accessibility specialist, SEO strategist, and senior front-end engineer for a complete redesign of the Memory Health Life Center website.

You are working directly inside the existing project repository.

Your job is not merely to restyle the current website.

Your job is to transform it into a polished, emotionally compelling, highly credible public website for a significant East Texas healthcare, philanthropic, educational, and community initiative.

You have authority to:

- redesign layouts
- restructure page hierarchy
- create new reusable components
- improve typography and spacing
- replace weak visual compositions
- redesign navigation
- simplify confusing content
- create new visual storytelling sections
- generate original supporting visual assets when appropriate
- generate illustrations, icons, diagrams, maps, textures, or other non-photographic assets when they materially improve the experience
- propose better use of existing project renderings and photography
- remove redundant or low-value UI
- improve responsive behavior
- improve accessibility
- improve SEO and AI discoverability
- improve performance
- improve donor conversion
- improve family usability

Do not treat the existing implementation as sacred.

However:

**DO NOT invent facts.**  
**DO NOT invent partnerships.**  
**DO NOT invent medical services.**  
**DO NOT invent funding commitments.**  
**DO NOT invent construction progress.**  
**DO NOT imply the Center is operational if it is not.**  
**DO NOT fabricate patient experiences.**  
**DO NOT create fake photographs purporting to show existing residents or an operational facility.**

Where facts are uncertain, flag them for verification.

The goal is a sophisticated, trustworthy website — not marketing exaggeration.

## 2. Core Project Positioning

The organization is the:

**MEMORY HEALTH LIFE CENTER**  
Quitman, Texas  
A project of the Wood County Health Care Foundation.

The current concept is a planned 54-resident memory-care campus in East Texas inspired by the Hogeweyk model.

The defining idea is not simply memory care.

The defining idea is:

**LIFE WITH DEMENTIA RATHER THAN CONFINEMENT BECAUSE OF DEMENTIA.**

The Center should be presented as a residential community designed around everyday life.

Residents are envisioned as living in homes, moving through outdoor spaces, maintaining routines, gardening, interacting with nature, participating in activities, sharing meals, and living within a recognizable community environment.

The website should make the distinction clear between:

> “an institution where people with dementia are managed”

and

> “a community where people with dementia continue living.”

This concept must become one of the strongest visual and emotional ideas in the entire website.

## 4. The Three Core Stories

The entire website should reinforce three connected narratives.

### Story 1 — A Better Way to Live With Dementia

Explain the residential-community model.

Show how environment, routine, freedom of movement, familiar spaces, outdoor life, social interaction, and person-centered care differ from a conventional institutional environment.

Do not rely exclusively on text.

Use visual storytelling.

Possible techniques:

- illustrated diagrams
- day-in-the-life storytelling
- campus diagrams
- visual comparisons
- resident-scale environments
- landscape imagery
- icons
- short narrative sequences
- architectural renderings

Make the concept understandable to someone who has never heard of Hogeweyk.

### Story 2 — A Regional Resource for East Texas

The project is more than residential beds.

Where supported by project materials, communicate its potential role in:

- caregiver education
- medical education
- healthcare training
- dementia knowledge
- workforce development
- family support
- brain-health programming
- rural healthcare capacity
- community education

Clearly distinguish planned functions from currently operating services.

Highlight supported relationships with:

- Quitman
- Wood County
- East Texas
- UT Health Quitman
- University of Texas at Tyler School of Medicine
- other verified healthcare, educational, governmental, or community partners

Position the project as regional healthcare infrastructure rather than merely a local facility.

### Story 3 — A Philanthropic Project With Momentum

Visitors should not perceive this as a vague future concept.

Communicate tangible momentum.

Where supported by project records, show:

- project origins
- planning
- land
- design
- partnerships
- federal support
- public-sector support
- fundraising progress
- institutional endorsements
- architectural development
- current funding need
- next milestones

The desired emotional response is:

**“This is real.  
This is important.  
This is moving forward.  
My involvement could help make it happen.”**

## 5. Homepage Experience

The homepage should behave like a narrative journey.

Avoid building a long stack of unrelated marketing cards.

Create strong pacing.

Recommended story sequence:

### A. Hero

Immediately establish:

- what this is
- where it is
- why it is different

Preferred headline direction:

**A new kind of memory care for East Texas.**

Do not overcomplicate the headline.

Supporting copy should quickly explain that the Memory Health Life Center is being developed as a residential community where people living with dementia can continue experiencing daily life with dignity, purpose, familiarity, and connection.

Provide two strong paths:

**EXPLORE THE CENTER**

**HELP BUILD IT**

Donation should remain prominent, but the first impression should be about the mission, not asking for money.

Use the strongest available project imagery.

If the architectural rendering is the best visual asset, use it confidently.

Do not visually imply the campus already exists if the image is a rendering.

Label renderings where appropriate.

Possible credibility indicators near the hero:

- 54 residents
- 29 acres
- $6 million federal funding secured
- East Texas regional training resource

Verify all facts before publishing.

### B. The Human Need

Begin with the experience of East Texas families.

Possible themes:

- long distances to specialists
- family caregivers carrying enormous responsibility
- limited rural dementia resources
- few alternatives to institutional models
- aging rural populations

Use statistics only after establishing the human story.

Every major healthcare statistic should have an identifiable source.

### C. A Different Way to Live

This should be one of the signature sections.

Consider creating a visual concept such as:

**A Day at the Memory Health Life Center**

- Morning at home
- Walking outdoors
- Gardening
- Lunch with neighbors
- Activities
- Family visits
- Evening routine

This should feel calm and dignified.

Do not infantilize residents.

Do not use cartoonish senior-care graphics.

If an illustration system would improve this section, create an original visual system consistent with the website brand.

### D. How the Model Is Different

Explain the difference visually and concisely.

Possible contrast:

**Traditional institutional model**  
versus  
**Residential community model**

Avoid attacking existing healthcare providers.

Frame it positively around what this project is designed to accomplish.

### E. More Than a Residential Campus

Show broader impact.

Potential pillars:

- Residential Memory Care
- Family & Caregiver Support
- Medical & Clinical Education
- Healthcare Workforce Development
- Brain Health Programs
- Research & Learning

Only include categories supported by project plans.

Mark future concepts appropriately.

### F. Built in Quitman. Built for East Texas.

Show regional relevance.

Consider generating a clean, sophisticated map illustrating:

- Quitman
- Wood County
- East Texas
- major nearby communities
- healthcare and educational relationships

The visual should reinforce regional importance rather than merely showing location.

### G. Proof

At the point where a skeptical visitor might ask:

**“Is this project real?”**

provide credibility.

Possible proof elements:

- university relationships
- letters of support
- medical leaders
- public funding
- project renderings
- press coverage
- community support
- governmental support

Avoid a giant logo wall.

Curate evidence.

### H. Project Progress

Create an elegant timeline.

Clearly separate:

**COMPLETED**  
**CURRENTLY UNDERWAY**  
**NEXT**

Do not visually represent planned milestones as accomplished.

The timeline should be easy to update later.

### I. Philanthropic Opportunity

Transition naturally from impact to participation.

Do not suddenly insert a generic donation widget.

Explain what philanthropy enables.

Possible pathways:

- Make a Gift
- Major Gifts
- Naming Opportunities
- Corporate & Foundation Giving
- Legacy Giving
- Honor / Memorial Giving

Only implement giving options supported by Foundation operations.

### J. Final CTA

Create a strong closing statement.

Preferred conceptual direction:

**Help build a place where life continues.**

Possible actions:

- MAKE A GIFT
- LEARN MORE
- TALK WITH US

## 7. Family Journey

Treat families as a dedicated experience.

A family member visiting this site may be:

- worried
- exhausted
- uncertain
- researching options
- trying to understand dementia
- trying to determine whether this project will eventually serve their family

Do not overwhelm them immediately with funding information or institutional language.

The family journey should help answer:

- What is this place?
- How would it be different?
- What would daily life look like?
- Who would it serve?
- Is it open yet?
- When might services become available?
- Can I receive updates?
- Where can I find reliable information now?

If the Center is not operational, make that clear without diminishing hope.

---

## 8. Donor Journey

Treat donors as a distinct audience.

A prospective donor needs answers to:

- Why is this needed?
- Why this model?
- Why East Texas?
- Why this organization?
- Is the project credible?
- What has already been accomplished?
- What remains unfunded?
- What will my gift help accomplish?
- Who else supports this?
- How do I discuss a significant gift?

Small online donors and major donors require different paths.

Do not force someone considering a six- or seven-figure contribution through the same user flow as someone making a $25 gift.

Provide an elegant pathway such as:

**Considering a significant gift? Let's talk.**

Preserve or refine:

**a respected healthcare foundation**  
+  
**a thoughtful residential community**

- gratuitous gradients
- overly rounded everything
- excessive drop shadows

- decorative UI without meaning

- What does person-centered dementia care mean?
- How does residential-style memory care differ?
- What should families look for?

Educational content should:

- answer real questions
- reduce uncertainty
- use reliable sources

## 13. Accessibility

Treat accessibility as a core design standard.

Target **WCAG 2.2 AA**.

Pay particular attention to older visitors and caregivers.

Implement:

- keyboard accessibility
- visible focus states
- skip navigation
- semantic landmarks
- strong heading hierarchy
- adequate contrast
- accessible menu behavior
- descriptive links
- good alt text
- accessible forms
- accessible error messages
- sufficiently large tap targets
- readable body text
- reasonable line lengths
- reduced-motion support
- screen-reader-friendly component structure

Do not sacrifice readability for visual minimalism.

---

## 14. Privacy

Audit every place where visitor data is collected.

Avoid requesting medical information unnecessarily.

Review:

- contact forms
- family-interest forms
- newsletter forms
- donation systems
- prospective resident forms
- referral forms
- analytics
- third-party scripts
- embedded media
- tracking pixels

If a form could collect sensitive healthcare data, flag it for privacy review before implementation.

Do not casually add tracking technology.

---

## 15. Performance

The site should work well on rural mobile networks.

Optimize aggressively but sensibly.

Do not ship giant base64 images in production HTML.

Use:

- properly sized responsive images
- modern formats when appropriate
- lazy loading
- efficient fonts
- minimal JavaScript
- CSS rather than JS for simple interaction
- image optimization
- code splitting when justified

Protect Core Web Vitals.

Avoid adding large libraries simply for small visual effects.

## 20. Visual QA Requirement

Do not assume that valid code equals good design.

After implementing each major page, inspect the actual rendered result.

Look specifically for:

- sections that feel templated
- excessive repetition
- weak visual hierarchy
- too many boxes
- awkward line lengths
- inconsistent margins
- poor mobile crops
- typography that feels too small
- oversized hero areas
- generic AI-generated design patterns
- unnecessary decorative effects
- content that feels dense
- donor CTAs that overpower the mission
- mission content that hides the donor path

Iterate visually.

If a section looks generic, redesign it.

---

## 21. Design Restraint

Do not add visual elements merely because you can generate them.

Every asset should serve one of four purposes:

**EXPLAIN**  
**ORIENT**  
**BUILD TRUST**  
**CREATE EMOTIONAL CONNECTION**

If it does none of those, remove it.

## 22. Definition of Success

A new visitor should understand within roughly 30 seconds:

- What is the Memory Health Life Center?
- Where is it?
- Why is it needed?
- Why is its model different?
- Is it operational yet?
- Why should I believe the project is credible?
- How far has it progressed?
- How can I participate?

Different audiences should leave with different reactions.

### A Family Member

> “This gives me hope, and I understand what they are trying to build.”

### A Physician

> “This is thoughtful and credible.”

### A University

> “This could become a meaningful educational partner.”

### A Policymaker

> “This is regional healthcare infrastructure.”

### A Community Member

> “This matters to East Texas.”

### A Major Donor

> “This is a serious project with enough vision and credibility to deserve a conversation.”

The central idea the entire website should reinforce is:

> **The Memory Health Life Center is not being built simply to care for people with dementia. It is being built to change what life with dementia can look like in East Texas.**
